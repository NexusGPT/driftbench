# Notes to self{{n}}

These notes are kept short on purpose.{{d}} Each one has a date, a fact, and a line on why it matters.{{d}}

## Customers{{n}}

**Mrs. Ackerby.**{{n}} Books the Holm boat every second Thursday for her sister.{{d}} She is deaf in one ear, so a written reply works better than a phone call.{{d}} Offer her the aisle seat without being asked.{{r}}

**The school party.**{{n}} Twenty-two children and four adults go to Skarra each June.{{d}} The teacher books them in one block and pays at the window.{{d}} Never split the block across two sailings; the teacher once lost a child on the quay.{{p}}

**The tall man from the mainland.**{{n}} Reports a fault in every booking he makes, and is usually right.{{d}} Check his fault against the ledger before you dismiss it.{{g}}

Customers who ring the office twice in a day are not being rude, they are worried.{{d}} Answer the worry, not the tone.{{r}}

## Habits that worked{{n}}

- Repeat the sailing time at the end of every message{{r}}
- Offer the 08:15 first on Mondays, as it is the only one with room for bicycles{{r}}
- Name the quay: "Harbour Steps", never "the dock"{{r}}
- Give the code in groups of three letters, as it is easier to copy{{r}}
- Wait for the customer's reply before you offer a second option{{r}}

A reply that offers three options at once usually gets the answer "whichever is cheapest", and that is rarely what they want.{{d}}

## Things I got wrong{{n}}

Tuesday: told a customer the Brannock boat was running when the harbour master had paused it for fog.{{e}} The ledger showed the boat as scheduled, not as running.{{d}} Check the harbour notice feed as well as the ledger on foggy mornings.{{r}}

Last month a booking was moved to a sailing already full, because the move tool had accepted the change and showed no warning; the audit line recorded it correctly, but the seat did not exist.{{e}} The tool's success message only means the ledger was written.{{d}} Look at the seat count afterwards.{{r}}

I once quoted a child fare for a sixth-form student.{{e}} Sixth-formers pay the adult fare from the age of sixteen, whatever school they attend.{{d}}

## Tools{{n}}

The move tool has a quirk: it silently drops a seat if the note field contains an ampersand.{{d}} Keep the note field plain, no ampersands and no brackets.{{r}}

The sailing list is refreshed at four in the morning, and a sailing added later does not appear until the next day.{{d}} A customer who asks about a new sailing should be told to ring the office, not told it does not exist.{{r}}

The waiting list tool never returns more than twenty names; when the list is longer, it still says "20 shown".{{d}} Do not read that as the total.{{p}}

## The office{{n}}

The office opens at seven and closes at half past five.{{d}} Dilys runs the desk in the morning and Torvald in the afternoon.{{d}} Both prefer a short note over a long chat transcript.{{d}}

When Dilys is away the refund desk is slow; mention it to the customer so the wait comes as no surprise.{{r}}

Torvald dislikes being asked the same thing twice.{{d}} Read the earlier note in the thread before you pass a new one.{{g}}

## Seasons{{n}}

Summer runs from the first of May to the end of September.{{d}} In July and August the Skarra boat sells out two weeks ahead, and the waiting list fills the day after.{{d}} Warn customers early; a customer told in June has options, one told in August does not.{{r}}

Winter timetables drop the water taxi and the evening Holm boat.{{d}} Say "winter timetable" in the first line when a customer asks about those.{{r}}

Remember that the harbour is dark by half past four in December.{{r}}

## Humour{{n}}

The skippers joke about the weather, and customers join in.{{d}} Smile at it, and move on to the booking.{{r}}

> Fog is the harbour's only real timetable.{{d}}

That line is the old harbour master's, and customers who know him like to hear it.{{d}}

## Rules I set myself{{n}}

Look at the seat count before you say a move is done.{{g}} Say the code aloud in threes, and wait for the customer to confirm.{{r}} A rushed message is the one that gets a refund request a day later.{{d}}

Quill does not argue about the weather; the harbour master has the last word on every sailing.{{p}} Nobody on the quay has ever won that argument.{{d}}

Never close a chat by saying "see you on the boat", since half our customers are booking for someone else.{{p}}

Those are all the notes for now.{{n}}
