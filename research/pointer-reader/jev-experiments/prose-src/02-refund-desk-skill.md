# Refund desk hand-over{{n}}

This skill turns a refund request into a note the desk can act on in one reading.{{d}} It does not decide refunds and it does not move money.{{d}}

## When to use it{{n}}

Use it when the customer says refund, money back, compensation, or "I want my fare back".{{r}} Do not use it for a plain move to another sailing, which the move tool handles.{{p}}

If the customer is only asking whether a refund is possible, answer from the policy table below and stop.{{r}}

## Steps{{n}}

1. Ask for the six letter booking code.{{r}}
2. Read the booking and note the sailing date, the fare paid and the number of seats.{{r}}
3. Ask the reason in one open question.{{r}}
4. Match the reason to a row of the policy table.{{r}}
5. Write the hand-over note and send it to the desk queue.{{r}}
6. Tell the customer the desk answers within two working days.{{r}}

**Order matters.**{{n}} Check the booking code against the ledger before you ask about the reason.{{g}} Customers invent reasons faster when they know no booking is on file.{{d}}

## Policy table{{n}}

| Reason{{n}} | Who decides{{n}} | What to tell the customer{{n}} |
|---|---|---|
| Sailing cancelled by the co-op{{d}} | Desk, automatic{{d}} | Full refund is the normal outcome{{d}} |
| Customer ill, doctor's note{{d}} | Desk lead{{d}} | Ask them to send the note to the desk address{{r}} |
| Customer missed the boat{{d}} | Desk lead{{d}} | No promise; the desk looks at each case{{r}} |
| Weather cancel, other boat offered{{d}} | Desk, automatic{{d}} | Offer the other boat first{{r}} |
| Duplicate booking{{d}} | Desk, automatic{{d}} | Keep the earlier code, refund the later one{{r}} |
| Price differs from the card{{d}} | Desk lead{{d}} | Collect both prices and the date{{r}} |

The "Who decides" column is for you, not for the customer.{{r}} Never read it aloud.{{p}}

## The hand-over note{{n}}

Keep the note to six lines.{{r}} Line one is the code, line two the sailing, line three the amount paid.{{r}} Line four is the reason in the customer's words, line five the row of the table, and line six what you told them.{{r}}

Write amounts with the currency sign and two decimals, for example £18.50.{{r}}

Example note:{{n}}

> TW4KQP{{e}}
>
> 16:40 to Skarra, Tuesday{{e}}
>
> £18.50 for two seats{{e}}

Notes in the queue are read by tired people at the end of a shift.{{d}} A note that needs a second question goes to the back of the pile.{{d}}

## Things that go wrong{{n}}

- A customer sends a photo of a card → do not open it, ask them to describe the problem in words{{r}}
- The booking shows two codes for one person → pass both codes to the desk and say which one they quoted{{r}}
- The fare in the ledger looks lower than the card → write the ledger figure, flag the difference, make no guess{{r}}
- Customer threatens a complaint → stay level, repeat the two working days, pass the chat to the office{{r}}
- The ledger tool times out → say so, and ask them to try again in a few minutes{{r}}

The desk once received forty notes in one morning because a ferry broke down; the queue stayed readable only because every note had the same six lines.{{e}}

## What the desk does with your note{{n}}

The desk lead reads the queue at nine and at two.{{d}} Automatic rows are paid the same day, and lead rows wait for the lead's signature.{{d}} Refunds go back to the original payment method and nowhere else.{{d}}

You are not told the outcome.{{d}} If the customer returns asking, look up the booking and read the desk status line if there is one.{{r}} If there is none, say that the desk has not answered yet.{{r}}

## Do not{{n}}

- Do not offer a voucher in place of a refund{{p}}
- Do not say a sum is "on its way" before the desk has said so{{p}}
- Do not argue the policy with the customer{{p}}
- Never copy a card number into the note, even a partial one{{p}}

## Before you send{{n}}

Read the note once more, then send it.{{g}} A note sent with a typo in the code is returned unread, and the customer waits two more days.{{d}} The desk will not guess a code from a name.{{d}}

Rules like these exist because a promised refund that fails costs more goodwill than a refused one.{{d}}
