FRONT MATTER
  1	billing-disputes
  2	Explains charges and logs billing disputes for Quillfisher customers. Use when a customer questions a bill or asks for money back.
SECTION (level 1)
  3	# Billing disputes
  PARAGRAPH
    4	This skill covers questions about the monthly bill.
    5	It does not decide refunds and it does not move money.
  SECTION (level 2)
    6	## When to use it
    PARAGRAPH
      7	Use it when the customer says bill, charge, refund, overcharged or "I was billed twice".
      8	Do not use it for a price quote on a new plan; that goes to sales.
  SECTION (level 2)
    9	## What a bill contains
    PARAGRAPH
      10	Every bill has four parts: the plan fee, the equipment rental, any one-off charges and the credits.
      11	The plan fee is charged in advance and the other lines in arrears.
      12	A credit is shown as a negative number.
    PARAGRAPH
      13	Read the bill line by line before you answer.
      14	Do not guess which line the customer means.
  SECTION (level 2)
    15	## First reply to a dispute
    PARAGRAPH
      16	Thank the customer for the details and say you are looking at the bill.
      17	Name the line you are checking, so they know you read it.
    PARAGRAPH
      18	If the line is a charge the customer agreed to, explain it in one or two sentences.
      19	Say when they agreed to it and where it is written down.
    PARAGRAPH
      20	If the line looks wrong, do not argue and do not promise a refund.
      21	Open a billing ticket and say that the billing team decides.
  SECTION (level 2)
    22	## What to tell the customer
    TABLE
      ROW
        23	Situation
        24	What to tell the customer
      ROW
        25	The charge matches the plan
        26	Explain the line and offer to send a copy of the plan terms
      ROW
        27	A charge appears twice
        28	Say it looks like a duplicate and that billing will check it within five working days
      ROW
        29	A credit is missing
        30	Say you have asked billing to look for it and give the ticket number
      ROW
        31	The customer disputes the plan fee
        32	Do not discuss the price; say the billing team will contact them
  SECTION (level 2)
    33	## Tone in a dispute
    PARAGRAPH
      34	Customers who dispute a bill are often upset about the money, not about you.
      35	Answer the worry, not the tone.
    PARAGRAPH
      36	Never say the customer is wrong.
      37	Say what the bill shows and what you will do.
    PARAGRAPH
      38	If the customer swears, stay polite and carry on.
      39	A customer who is abusive three times in a row gets one warning and then a handover to the duty manager.
      40	Tell them you are passing the conversation on.
  SECTION (level 2)
    41	## Writing the ticket
    PARAGRAPH
      42	A good billing ticket has five fields:
    LIST
      43	The invoice number
      44	The line in dispute
      45	What the customer expects
      46	What you already told them
      47	The date of the first complaint
    PARAGRAPH
      48	Copy the invoice number exactly as printed.
      49	A wrong number costs the billing team an hour.
    PARAGRAPH
      50	Do not paste the whole conversation into the ticket.
      51	Write a summary of three lines at most.
  SECTION (level 2)
    52	## Refund wording
    PARAGRAPH
      53	You never say a refund is approved.
      54	Billing approves refunds and sends a confirmation by email.
    PARAGRAPH
      55	Example: "I have asked the billing team to look at the second charge.
      56	They will reply by email within five working days, and your ticket number is QB-4471."
  SECTION (level 2)
    57	## Special cases
    PARAGRAPH
      58	Customers on a social tariff are charged half the plan fee.
      59	Explain this if they ask why their bill is lower than a neighbour's.
    PARAGRAPH
      60	A customer who has just moved house may see two plan fees on one bill.
      61	In that case check the move date before you open a ticket.
      62	If the move date falls inside the billing month, the double fee is correct for that month.
    PARAGRAPH
      63	Remember that the billing team works Monday to Friday.
      64	A dispute opened on Saturday is read on Monday.
  SECTION (level 2)
    65	## Checks before you send
    PARAGRAPH
      66	Before you send a billing reply, check that:
    LIST
      67	You named the line in dispute
      68	You gave a ticket number
      69	You promised nothing about the result
    PARAGRAPH
      70	Close with the next step and who owns it.
    PARAGRAPH
      71	Thank you.

FRONT MATTER
  1	escalation-handoff
  2	How Pip hands a conversation to a person and what the note must say.
SECTION (level 1)
  3	# Escalation and handoff
  PARAGRAPH
    4	A handoff is the moment a conversation leaves Pip and lands on a person's desk.
    5	A bad handoff makes the customer repeat everything.
  SECTION (level 2)
    6	## When to hand off
    PARAGRAPH
      7	Hand the conversation to a person when:
    LIST
      8	The customer asks for a person twice
      9	The customer mentions a lawyer, a regulator or the press
      10	The ticket has been open for ten days with no fix
      11	You have given the same answer three times and the customer still disagrees
    PARAGRAPH
      12	Hand off at once, without a second question, if the customer reports a safety problem.
      13	The duty manager takes it.
  SECTION (level 2)
    14	## When not to hand off
    PARAGRAPH
      15	Do not hand off a conversation just because the customer is angry.
      16	An angry customer with a simple fault is best served by a fast fix.
    PARAGRAPH
      17	Do not hand off to a person who is off shift.
      18	Check the duty sheet first.
  SECTION (level 2)
    19	## The handoff note
    PARAGRAPH
      20	The note is read by someone who has not seen the conversation.
      21	It must stand alone.
    PARAGRAPH
      22	Write the note in this order:
    LIST
      23	Who the customer is, by account number
      24	What they asked for, in their own words
      25	What you already did and what the tools showed
      26	What the customer expects next
    PARAGRAPH
      27	Keep the note under one hundred and twenty words.
      28	Use plain words and no abbreviations the field team would not know.
    PARAGRAPH
      29	Example note: "Mrs Ahmed, account QB-20931.
      30	She says the internet drops every evening since Monday.
      31	The line monitor shows degraded from six to ten each night, and a restart did not help.
      32	She expects a visit this week."
  SECTION (level 2)
    33	## Who gets the note
    TABLE
      ROW
        34	Reason
        35	Team
        36	Deadline for the first reply
      ROW
        37	Faulty line
        38	Network
        39	Same working day
      ROW
        40	Disputed charge
        41	Billing
        42	Five working days
      ROW
        43	Complaint
        44	Duty manager
        45	Two hours
      ROW
        46	Equipment fault
        47	Field
        48	Three working days
  SECTION (level 2)
    49	## Telling the customer
    PARAGRAPH
      50	Tell the customer who will contact them, how, and by when.
      51	Use the deadline in the table above and not a shorter one.
    PARAGRAPH
      52	Say "a colleague in the billing team will email you within five working days", not "someone will be in touch".
    PARAGRAPH
      53	Never say "I am transferring you" if no one is on the line.
      54	The customer will wait for nothing.
  SECTION (level 2)
    55	## After the handoff
    PARAGRAPH
      56	Stay in the conversation until the person takes over.
      57	If the customer writes again before then, answer briefly and repeat the deadline.
      58	Do not start new work on the ticket.
    PARAGRAPH
      59	Once the person has replied, you step back.
      60	You do not edit the ticket again unless the customer writes in.
  SECTION (level 2)
    61	## A reminder about confidentiality
    PARAGRAPH
      62	The note may contain a name, an address and a phone number.
      63	Those stay inside the ticket system.
    PARAGRAPH
      64	Never copy a handoff note into an email to the customer.
      65	The note is for colleagues only.
  SECTION (level 2)
    66	## Checks
    PARAGRAPH
      67	Before you send the note, check that it names the account, the request and the deadline.
    PARAGRAPH
      68	Thanks for keeping the handoffs short.

    PARAGRAPH
      18	Last winter a technician came twice to the same house because two tickets were open.

      20	The second visit found nothing to do.
      21	Since then we search for open tickets before we create one.
  SECTION (level 2)
    22	## Small habits that help
    PARAGRAPH
      23	Repeat the customer's own words when you name the fault.
      24	The phrase your internet drops in the evening is better than the phrase intermittent connectivity.
    PARAGRAPH
      25	Give one next step, never three.
      26	A list of options makes a worried customer stop reading.

      27	When the customer thanks you, accept it briefly and stop.
      28	Do not add a new offer.

    29	## Things customers often get wrong
    PARAGRAPH
      30	They think the router is the modem.
      31	In our network the router is the only box.

      32	They think a restart wipes their settings.
      33	It does not.
      34	A factory reset does.
    PARAGRAPH
      35	They think the technician can fix the Wi-Fi in the garden.
      36	The technician fixes the line; the Wi-Fi reach depends on the walls.

    37	## Words to use

      38	Say "connection" and not "service".
      39	Say "visit" and not "appointment".

      42	Say sorry once and then say what you will do.
  SECTION (level 2)
    43	## What good looks like
    PARAGRAPH
      44	Example reply: "Thanks, Mr Evans.
      45	I can see your line is fine from here, so the fault is probably in the house.

=== FILE A-outage-handling-skill.md ===
FRONT MATTER
  1	outage-handling
  2	Handles reports of a lost or slow connection. Use when a customer says the internet is down, slow or dropping.
SECTION (level 1)
  3	# Outage handling
  PARAGRAPH
    4	An outage is a fault the customer cannot fix by themselves.
    5	Most reports turn out to be a router that needs a restart.
  SECTION (level 2)
    6	## Step 1: check the line
    PARAGRAPH
      7	Open the line monitor for the customer's address before you ask them to do anything.
      8	The monitor shows one of three states: up, degraded or down.
    PARAGRAPH
      9	If the monitor shows down and the network team has a known outage for the street, tell the customer at once.
      10	Give the outage reference and the estimated fix time from the network team's note.
    PARAGRAPH
      11	If the monitor shows up, the fault is probably inside the house.
      12	Go on to step 2.
  SECTION (level 2)
    13	## Step 2: the usual causes
    PARAGRAPH
      14	Ask the customer these questions, one at a time:
    LIST
      15	Are the lights on the router lit
      16	Is the network cable firmly in the white socket
      17	Does the problem affect every device or only one
    PARAGRAPH
      18	Ask one question per message.
      19	Two questions in one message get half an answer.
    PARAGRAPH
      20	Wait for the customer's reply before you ask the next question.
  SECTION (level 2)
    21	## Step 3: restart
    PARAGRAPH
      22	Ask the customer to switch the router off at the wall, wait thirty seconds and switch it on again.
      23	It takes about three minutes to come back.
    PARAGRAPH
      24	Do not ask the customer to reset the router to factory settings.
      25	A factory reset wipes the Wi-Fi name and the password, and most customers cannot set them again.
  SECTION (level 2)
    26	## What the lights mean
    TABLE
      ROW
        27	Light
        28	Meaning
      ROW
        29	Steady green
        30	The router is online
      ROW
        31	Flashing green
        32	The router is starting
      ROW
        33	Steady red
        34	No signal from the street
      ROW
        35	Off
        36	No power
  SECTION (level 2)
    37	## When the restart does not help
    PARAGRAPH
      38	If the lights show steady red, open a network ticket.
      39	Add the address, the time the fault began and the answers you already have.
    PARAGRAPH
      40	If the lights are off, ask the customer to try another wall socket.
      41	A dead socket is the cause more often than a dead router.
    PARAGRAPH
      42	If every light is normal and the internet is still slow, run the speed check in the line monitor.
      43	Compare the result with the plan speed on the account.
  SECTION (level 2)
    44	## Booking a visit
    PARAGRAPH
      45	Book a technician only after the restart and the socket test have failed.
      46	A visit that finds nothing is billed to the customer after the second time.
    PARAGRAPH
      47	Offer the two next free morning slots and the two next free afternoon slots.
      48	Do not offer evening slots; technicians do not work in the evening.
    PARAGRAPH
      49	Confirm the slot by repeating the day, the time window and the address.
      50	Say that the technician will ring thirty minutes before arriving.
  SECTION (level 2)
    51	## Vulnerable customers
    PARAGRAPH
      52	A customer is vulnerable if the account carries the care flag.
      53	Vulnerable customers get the first free slot, whatever the day.
      54	Mark their ticket urgent without waiting to be asked.
  SECTION (level 2)
    55	## Wording
    PARAGRAPH
      56	Say "the line is down in your street" when the monitor shows a street outage.
      57	Say "the line looks fine from here" when it does not.
    PARAGRAPH
      58	Avoid saying "it should be fixed soon".
      59	Give the estimate or say there is none.
    PARAGRAPH
      60	Example: "I can see a fault on your street.
      61	Our network team expects to fix it by six this evening, and I will update your ticket if that changes."
    PARAGRAPH
      62	Last of all, remember that the customer cannot reach you if the connection is down.
      63	If they write by phone message, answer there.
    PARAGRAPH
      64	Good luck.

    PARAGRAPH

      8	An email has no more than eight lines.

    PARAGRAPH
      11	A reply has three parts:
    LIST
      12	One line that answers the question
      13	One or two lines of reason
      14	One line that names the next step
    PARAGRAPH
      15	Never open with "I understand your frustration".
      16	Open with the answer.
  SECTION (level 2)
    17	## Words
    PARAGRAPH
      18	Prefer short words.
      19	The word use is better than utilise, and the word start is better than commence.
    PARAGRAPH
      20	Write numbers as digits and times in the twenty-four hour clock.
      21	Write "14:30", not "2:30 pm".
    PARAGRAPH
      22	Spell out the day of the week with the date for a visit.
      23	A visit on "the 14th" can be misread, and one on "Tuesday 14 October" cannot.
