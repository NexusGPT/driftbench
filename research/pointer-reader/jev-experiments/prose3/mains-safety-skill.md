# Mains safety

Mains electricity can kill, and a repair café is a busy and friendly place. The checks below stop the busy and friendly from getting in the way.

## Before a bench is set up

Before a mains item is opened, check all of these:

- The plug is out of the wall
- The cable has no cuts and no tape
- The item has stood unplugged for ten minutes
- The volunteer holds the mains badge

If any check fails, the item does not go on a bench. Give the visitor a note to take to a qualified electrician.

Capacitors hold a charge after the plug is out. Televisions, microwave ovens and camera flashes are the usual culprits.

Never open a microwave oven, however kindly the visitor asks and however experienced the volunteer.

The testing meter goes to mains-badge volunteers only; hand it to nobody else.

## Signs that something is wrong

| Sign | What it means | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Burning smell | An overheated part | Unplug at once and carry the item outside |
| Warm cable | An overloaded lead | Stop, and do not reuse the cable |
| Tripped breaker | A fault somewhere on the bench | Reset it once only, and only the session lead resets it |

The breaker board is in the porch cupboard and the key hangs beside it.

## If someone is hurt

If a visitor or a volunteer is hurt, stop everything else at the bench. Then send the first aider's name to the nearest volunteer and say where the person is. Stay with the person until the first aider arrives.

The first aider's number is on the lanyard board and on the café wall.

Call the emergency number yourself if the person is not breathing. Do not wait for the first aider to arrive.

## Resetting and restarting

Reset a tripped breaker once only. If it trips again, leave it alone and call the session lead.

Switch an item on only after the session lead has signed the ticket. A signature in the wrong box does not count, and the lead will ask for it again.

The fuse drawer holds fuses of 3, 5 and 13 amps. Fit a fuse of the same rating as the one you take out. Never fit a larger one to make a fault go away. Larger fuses hide faults, and the faults come back.

Case: in March a volunteer opened a camera flash and felt the charge in his thumb. He was unhurt, and the café now keeps a discharge stick in the porch cupboard.

## Training

The mains badge is a one-hour test taken twice a year at the library. Volunteers who fail may retake it after a month. The list of badge holders is pinned beside the breaker board.

Ask a volunteer to show their badge before you pass them a mains ticket. Do not take their word for it, and do not accept a badge from last year.

Stay safe.
