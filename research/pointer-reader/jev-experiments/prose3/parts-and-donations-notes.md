# Notes to self

These notes are short on purpose. Each has a date, a fact and a line on why it matters.

## Parts shelf

The parts shelf holds belts, fuses, switches, zips and about two hundred buttons. It is sorted by kind, not by size, and the label on each box is the only index.

Check the shelf before you tell a visitor that a part has to be ordered.

A visitor asks for a part we may have. Describe it to a volunteer in one line, with the item it came from, and wait for the volunteer to look.

Never promise a part. A box that was full on Saturday can be empty by Sunday, because the café lends parts to the library.

Case: a visitor was told by phone that a vacuum belt was in stock. She travelled forty minutes and the box held two belts for another model.

## Donations

Donated items arrive at the porch on weekday mornings. A trustee logs each one in the donations book and the tinkerers decide what to keep.

Accept a donation only if a trustee has agreed to it. If a visitor offers something at the door, take their name and number and say that a trustee will call.

Items we do not take: anything with a swollen battery, gas appliances, mattresses and chemicals. Say this kindly and early, so the visitor does not carry a heavy box for nothing.

Thank every donor in writing. A short note is enough: name the item, say what it will be used for and sign it with the café's name.

A donor asks for a receipt for tax. The café is not a charity for that purpose, so say so plainly and do not write anything that looks like a receipt.

## Habits that worked

- Say the session date at the end of every message
- Offer the earliest slot first
- Call the bike corner the bike corner, never the yard
- Give the ticket number in groups of two digits
- Wait for the visitor to answer before offering a second choice

A reply with three choices at once usually gets the answer whichever is quickest, and that is rarely what the visitor wants.

## Things I got wrong

Monday: I told a visitor the sewing table was free on Saturday. The calendar showed the table as booked by the library group, and I had read the wrong row. Read the row for the right date before you say a table is free.

Last month a ticket was closed with the outcome mended while the volunteer's note said part mended. The ticket tool saves whatever the last box says. Compare the note with the box before you read an outcome out loud.

I once gave a visitor a phone number from the volunteers' list. Volunteers are not asked whether their number may be shared, so no number from that list leaves the café.

## The people

Mrs Okafor brings her husband's radios every first Saturday and likes to sit near the window. Offer her the window bench without being asked.

The Penhale twins mend bicycles at the bike corner and answer no messages on Fridays. Do not ask them anything on a Friday.

Visitors who write twice in one day are not being rude; they are worried. Answer the worry, not the tone.

## Seasons

The café is busiest in October and November, when people bring heaters and lamps before the dark evenings. Sessions fill three weeks ahead, and the waiting list fills the next day. Say early that a slot may need a wait, because a visitor told in October has choices and one told in November does not.

In December the café opens on one Saturday only. Say "one session in December" the first time a visitor asks about it.

Remember that the porch is dark by four in December.

Those are all the notes for now.
