# Repair ticket

This skill creates the ticket that follows an item through the café. It does not book a bench and it does not promise a repair.

## Steps

1. Ask for the visitor's name and phone number.
2. Ask what the item is and what it did when it last worked.
3. Write the fields of the ticket in the order of the table below.
4. Read the ticket back to the visitor.
5. Give the visitor the ticket number.

**Numbering.** Number every ticket from the book, not from memory. The book holds the next number on the first line of the open page. Take that number, write it on the ticket, and cross it out of the book before you do anything else.

## What each kind of item needs

| Kind of item | You see | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Mains appliance | A plug or a cable | Book a bench with a volunteer who holds the mains badge |
| Battery item | A charger or a cell | Ask for the battery to be taken out before the session |
| Bicycle | Two wheels | Send the visitor to the bike corner, no ticket needed |
| Clothing | A seam, a zip or a button | Book the sewing table |

Photograph the item before anyone touches it. Take one picture of the whole item and one of the fault, and attach both to the ticket.

The photographs live on the café tablet and are deleted after ninety days.

## Awkward cases

- Visitor has no phone number → write the email address instead and tick the box "no phone"
- Item arrives with no visitor → do not make a ticket, put it on the shelf marked "waiting" and tell a volunteer
- Item is bigger than the benches → offer a house visit and take the address
- Visitor wants a ticket for a second item → write a second ticket with its own number
- Visitor cannot spell their name → ask for an email address and copy the name from it

The visitor says the item is a gift. Write the name of the person who will use it in the note box, and ask whether they will come to the session too.

Never change the owner on a ticket. The one exception is a family member who brings the item for the owner, and then the volunteer who mends it writes both names.

A visitor who has lost a ticket can read the number from their email. If they have no email, look the ticket up by name and the date of the session.

## What the volunteers do with your ticket

A volunteer picks up the ticket at the bench and writes the outcome on the back. The outcome is one of mended, part mended, not mendable or needs a part. Tickets marked needs a part go to the parts shelf and are kept for sixty days.

You can read the outcome once the ticket is closed. While a ticket is open, say that it is with the volunteers and do not guess.

Do not give out an outcome before the visitor has collected the item.

## Quality of the notes

Write the fault in the visitor's words, not in yours. A note such as "it makes a funny noise" is more use to a volunteer than "motor fault".

Notes are read by volunteers with greasy hands and ten minutes. A note that needs a second question goes to the bottom of the pile.

Good luck with the tickets.
