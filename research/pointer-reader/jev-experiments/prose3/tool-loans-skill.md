# Tool loans

The café owns about forty hand tools and power tools, and members may borrow them for a week. A membership costs six pounds a year and is paid at the door.

Do not lend a tool to a visitor who is not a member.

## Lending

Look the member up in the member list before you lend anything.

Write four things in the loan book:

- The member's name
- The tool and its number
- The date of the loan
- The date it is due back

A member asks for a power tool. Check the date of their safety sheet, and if it is older than a year, ask them to sign a new one before the loan.

Never lend the angle grinder. The one exception is a member with the grinder badge, and then only on the day of a session.

Lend no more than two tools to one member at a time.

The tool number is on a yellow tag, and the tag stays on the tool.

## Taking tools back

Check each tool when it comes back. Test the switch, wipe the blade and count the pieces against the list on the lid, then write checked and your initials on the loan line.

A tool comes back damaged. Take a photograph, tell the session lead, and mark the tool out of service in the book.

Tell the member to bring three things to the desk on the day a tool goes back:

- The tool itself, with its cord
- The yellow tag, if it has come off
- The loan slip they were given

## Late returns

The loan book shows which loans are late each Saturday morning. The session lead reads the list out at the start of the session.

| Days late | Then |
|---|---|
| One to seven | Send a reminder and charge nothing |
| Eight to fourteen | Charge one pound a day and say so in the message |
| More than fourteen | Pass the loan to the session lead |

Keep reminders polite and short. Name the tool, name the due date and ask when the member can bring it back.

Example: "Hello Sam, the cordless drill, tool 14, was due back on the 3rd. Could you bring it on Saturday, or tell me if you need another week?"

## Lost and broken

If a tool is lost, the member pays half the price of a new one. The session lead decides the sum and writes it in the loan book.

Do not quote a sum to the member yourself.

A member says a tool was already broken when they borrowed it. Do not argue. Look up the check line from the last return, and pass both to the session lead.

## Tool care

Power tools are kept in the locked cupboard and hand tools on the open rack. The cupboard key hangs in the porch beside the breaker key.

Keep blades dry and cords coiled loosely. A tight coil cracks the insulation over time.

Never leave a tool on a bench overnight. Put it back on the rack, or in the cupboard if it is a power tool.

The tool cupboard is checked by the session lead on the last Saturday of the month. Any tool missing from the list is written up and the member list is searched.

That is all for tool loans.
