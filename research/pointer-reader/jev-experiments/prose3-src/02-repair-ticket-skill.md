# Repair ticket{{n}}

This skill creates the ticket that follows an item through the café.{{d}} It does not book a bench and it does not promise a repair.{{d}}

## Steps{{n}}

1. Ask for the visitor's name and phone number.{{r}}
2. Ask what the item is and what it did when it last worked.{{r}}
3. Write the fields of the ticket in the order of the table below.{{r}}
4. Read the ticket back to the visitor.{{r}}
5. Give the visitor the ticket number.{{r}}

**Numbering.**{{n}} Number every ticket from the book, not from memory.{{r#a}} The book holds the next number on the first line of the open page.{{r#a}} Take that number, write it on the ticket, and cross it out of the book before you do anything else.{{r#a}}

## What each kind of item needs{{n}}

| Kind of item{{n}} | You see{{n}} | Then{{n}} |
|---|---|---|
| Mains appliance{{r#t1}} | A plug or a cable{{r#t1}} | Book a bench with a volunteer who holds the mains badge{{r#t1}} |
| Battery item{{r#t2}} | A charger or a cell{{r#t2}} | Ask for the battery to be taken out before the session{{r#t2}} |
| Bicycle{{r#t3}} | Two wheels{{r#t3}} | Send the visitor to the bike corner, no ticket needed{{r#t3}} |
| Clothing{{r#t4}} | A seam, a zip or a button{{r#t4}} | Book the sewing table{{r#t4}} |

Photograph the item before anyone touches it.{{r#b}} Take one picture of the whole item and one of the fault, and attach both to the ticket.{{r#b}}

The photographs live on the café tablet and are deleted after ninety days.{{d}}

## Awkward cases{{n}}

- Visitor has no phone number → write the email address instead and tick the box "no phone"{{r}}
- Item arrives with no visitor → do not make a ticket, put it on the shelf marked "waiting" and tell a volunteer{{p}}
- Item is bigger than the benches → offer a house visit and take the address{{r}}
- Visitor wants a ticket for a second item → write a second ticket with its own number{{r}}
- Visitor cannot spell their name → ask for an email address and copy the name from it{{r}}

The visitor says the item is a gift.{{r#c}} Write the name of the person who will use it in the note box, and ask whether they will come to the session too.{{r#c}}

Never change the owner on a ticket.{{p#d}} The one exception is a family member who brings the item for the owner, and then the volunteer who mends it writes both names.{{p#d}}

A visitor who has lost a ticket can read the number from their email.{{d}} If they have no email, look the ticket up by name and the date of the session.{{r}}

## What the volunteers do with your ticket{{n}}

A volunteer picks up the ticket at the bench and writes the outcome on the back.{{d}} The outcome is one of mended, part mended, not mendable or needs a part.{{d}} Tickets marked needs a part go to the parts shelf and are kept for sixty days.{{d}}

You can read the outcome once the ticket is closed.{{d}} While a ticket is open, say that it is with the volunteers and do not guess.{{p}}

Do not give out an outcome before the visitor has collected the item.{{p}}

## Quality of the notes{{n}}

Write the fault in the visitor's words, not in yours.{{r}} A note such as "it makes a funny noise" is more use to a volunteer than "motor fault".{{e}}

Notes are read by volunteers with greasy hands and ten minutes.{{d}} A note that needs a second question goes to the bottom of the pile.{{d}}

Good luck with the tickets.{{n}}
