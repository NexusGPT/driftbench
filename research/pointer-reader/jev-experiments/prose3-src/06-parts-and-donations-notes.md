# Notes to self{{n}}

These notes are short on purpose.{{d}} Each has a date, a fact and a line on why it matters.{{d}}

## Parts shelf{{n}}

The parts shelf holds belts, fuses, switches, zips and about two hundred buttons.{{d}} It is sorted by kind, not by size, and the label on each box is the only index.{{d}}

Check the shelf before you tell a visitor that a part has to be ordered.{{g}}

A visitor asks for a part we may have.{{r#a}} Describe it to a volunteer in one line, with the item it came from, and wait for the volunteer to look.{{r#a}}

Never promise a part.{{p}} A box that was full on Saturday can be empty by Sunday, because the café lends parts to the library.{{d}}

Case: a visitor was told by phone that a vacuum belt was in stock.{{e}} She travelled forty minutes and the box held two belts for another model.{{e}}

## Donations{{n}}

Donated items arrive at the porch on weekday mornings.{{d}} A trustee logs each one in the donations book and the tinkerers decide what to keep.{{d}}

Accept a donation only if a trustee has agreed to it.{{p#b}} If a visitor offers something at the door, take their name and number and say that a trustee will call.{{p#b}}

Items we do not take: anything with a swollen battery, gas appliances, mattresses and chemicals.{{d}} Say this kindly and early, so the visitor does not carry a heavy box for nothing.{{r}}

Thank every donor in writing.{{r#c}} A short note is enough: name the item, say what it will be used for and sign it with the café's name.{{r#c}}

A donor asks for a receipt for tax.{{p#d}} The café is not a charity for that purpose, so say so plainly and do not write anything that looks like a receipt.{{p#d}}

## Habits that worked{{n}}

- Say the session date at the end of every message{{r}}
- Offer the earliest slot first{{r}}
- Call the bike corner the bike corner, never the yard{{p}}
- Give the ticket number in groups of two digits{{r}}
- Wait for the visitor to answer before offering a second choice{{g}}

A reply with three choices at once usually gets the answer whichever is quickest, and that is rarely what the visitor wants.{{d}}

## Things I got wrong{{n}}

Monday: I told a visitor the sewing table was free on Saturday.{{e}} The calendar showed the table as booked by the library group, and I had read the wrong row.{{e}} Read the row for the right date before you say a table is free.{{g}}

Last month a ticket was closed with the outcome mended while the volunteer's note said part mended.{{e}} The ticket tool saves whatever the last box says.{{d}} Compare the note with the box before you read an outcome out loud.{{g}}

I once gave a visitor a phone number from the volunteers' list.{{e}} Volunteers are not asked whether their number may be shared, so no number from that list leaves the café.{{p}}

## The people{{n}}

Mrs Okafor brings her husband's radios every first Saturday and likes to sit near the window.{{d}} Offer her the window bench without being asked.{{r}}

The Penhale twins mend bicycles at the bike corner and answer no messages on Fridays.{{d}} Do not ask them anything on a Friday.{{p}}

Visitors who write twice in one day are not being rude; they are worried.{{d}} Answer the worry, not the tone.{{r}}

## Seasons{{n}}

The café is busiest in October and November, when people bring heaters and lamps before the dark evenings.{{d}} Sessions fill three weeks ahead, and the waiting list fills the next day.{{d}} Say early that a slot may need a wait, because a visitor told in October has choices and one told in November does not.{{r}}

In December the café opens on one Saturday only.{{d}} Say "one session in December" the first time a visitor asks about it.{{r}}

Remember that the porch is dark by four in December.{{r}}

Those are all the notes for now.{{n}}
