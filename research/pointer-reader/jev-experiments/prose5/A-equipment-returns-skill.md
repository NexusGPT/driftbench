# Equipment returns

Quillfisher lends a router to every customer and a set-top box to some. The equipment stays ours and must come back when the customer leaves.

## What counts as equipment

The list is short:

- The router
- The power adapter of the router
- The set-top box and its remote
- The white wall socket cover, if it was fitted by us

Cables that the customer bought themselves are theirs to keep.

## Starting a return

Check that the customer has told us they are leaving or upgrading. Never start a return because a customer sounds unhappy.

Ask which pieces of equipment they still have at home. Write them down one by one in the ticket.

Offer the free return label first. If the customer cannot print it, offer a drop-off at the post office on Mill Lane instead.

## The return label

The label is sent by email and is valid for thirty days. It can be used once.

Tell the customer to pack the equipment in the original box if they have it. If they have no box, any sturdy box will do.

Remind them to unplug the router only after the new connection works. A customer who unplugs early loses their service for the gap.

## What to do for each situation

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| The customer has the original box | Send the label and say the box is enough |
| The customer lost the power adapter | Note it on the ticket and say a fee may apply |
| The customer lost the router | Do not promise to waive the fee; send the ticket to billing |
| The router is damaged | Ask for a photo and attach it to the ticket |

## Charges

An unreturned router is charged at forty pounds after sixty days. A set-top box is charged at sixty pounds. The charge is added to the final bill.

Do not quote these amounts as a threat. Mention them only if the customer asks what happens when equipment is lost.

You cannot waive a charge. Only the billing team can.

## Tracking a return

Once the parcel is posted, the tracking number appears on the ticket within two days. If the customer asks where it is, look it up. Do not tell them it is lost until five working days have passed.

When the warehouse scans the parcel, the ticket closes by itself. You do not close return tickets by hand.

## A note on tone

A customer who is leaving is still a customer. Thank them for their time with us, and never try to talk them out of leaving.

If they ask why we want the router back, say plainly that it is ours and that we reuse it.

## Checks before you finish

Before you end the conversation, make sure that:

- The customer has a label or a drop-off address
- The ticket lists every piece of equipment
- You told them the thirty-day limit

Wish them well.

Thank you.
