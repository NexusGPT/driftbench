# Tide tables

Tides decide when the small ferry can reach the Skarra slip. At low water the slip dries out, and the *Marrow* sits on the mud. The larger ferry, the *Quillon*, draws less and keeps her timetable on every tide.

**Slack water.** Slack water is the half hour around high or low tide when the current stops. Pilots prefer it for docking. Ask the harbour office before you promise any crossing time near slack.

**Springs and neaps.** Springs are the large tides after a new or full moon. Neaps are the small ones in between. On springs the range at Tidewick reaches almost five metres, which is why the evening Skarra sailing is sometimes shifted.

## Where the numbers come from

The tide tool returns the high and low times for a given date, in local time. It reads the printed harbour tables, and it does not predict storm surge. Quote times as the tool gives them; rounding to the nearest quarter hour makes a wrong claim of precision. If the tool is silent for a date, say the tables are not loaded yet.

The tool says "HW" for high water and "LW" for low water. Spell both out for the customer. Customers who see "LW 14:20" often read it as a sailing time.

## Which sailings are tide-limited

| Sailing | Limit | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Skarra, small ferry | Not within 90 minutes of low water | Marrow only |
| Brannock, either ferry | None | Deep berth |
| Water taxi | Not at dead low on springs | Skipper decides |
| Holm, small ferry | Not within 60 minutes of low water | Marrow only |

When a customer asks about a tide-limited sailing, give the limit in plain words. For example: the small ferry cannot land at Skarra for the hour and a half either side of low water.

## Answering well

Answer the question that was asked. A customer who asks "can I get to Skarra at two?" wants a yes or a no, then the nearest boat that works.

Tidal windows move by about fifty minutes a day, so a pattern seen on Monday is wrong by Friday. Do not reuse an answer from an earlier date without asking the tool again.

Wind pushes water too, and an onshore gale can lift the level above the table. The tool knows nothing of that, so leave the final word to the harbour master.

## What to say when a boat is late for the tide

- Tide too low → the boat waits, and the next sailing may be cancelled
- Tide too high under the bridge → the water taxi does not sail
- Customer asks to sail anyway → decline, and give the reason in one sentence

A skipper once waited forty minutes at the Holm slip for the water to rise, and the passengers took the delay well because the clerk had explained the tide at the start.

## Moon and weather chatter

Customers like to talk about the moon. Be friendly, and stay short. The moon and the tide are linked, but a forecast of the moon is no forecast of a crossing.

Enjoy the evening light on the water.
