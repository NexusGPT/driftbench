# Lost property

**What it covers.** Bags, coats, phones, bicycle lights and the odd lifejacket left behind by walkers. Cash and passports are not covered; they go straight to the office.

**The shelf.** The office keeps found items on the blue shelf for ninety days. After that they go to the island charity shop.

## Logging a lost item

Ask for four things: what it is, where it was lost, on which sailing, and how to reach the owner. Write them into the lost log in that order.

Describe the item the way the customer does, not the way you would. A "navy bag" and a "dark blue holdall" are the same bag, and the shelf clerk searches by the customer's words.

- Item described in the customer's words
- Place and sailing
- Contact by email or phone, whichever they prefer
- Date, written the day the item was lost
- Any marks, such as a name tag or a broken zip

## Matching

A match needs three points to agree: the kind of item, the colour, and the sailing. Two points make a maybe, and you pass a maybe to the office rather than tell the customer it is found.

Do not describe a found item in more detail than the customer did. Someone who claims a bag will volunteer the details, and a stranger will simply agree with yours.

Never promise that an item is on the shelf until the clerk has held it in their hands.

## Handing back

Hand-back happens at the office window, in person, with identification. Ask the customer to bring a photo or a receipt if the item is worth more than a night out. The clerk signs the log line and the customer signs beside it.

For a customer who cannot come in, the office posts small items at the customer's cost. Quote the postage from the office, not from memory.

## Awkward cases

- A child's toy found with no owner → log it, and keep it on the blue shelf for the full ninety days
- A phone with a lock screen → do not try to unlock it, hand it to the office
- A bag with medicine in it → tell the office at once, as the owner may need it that day
- Two people claim the same bag → pass both names to the office and stop
- Item found on the quay, not aboard → log the quay and the time, and say it was found on the quay

Case: a coat left on the Marrow was matched on colour and sailing alone, handed to the wrong walker, and the real owner came the next morning to an empty hook.

## Wording

Say "we have logged your item", not "we will find your item". Logging is a promise we can keep.

The first message to a customer who has lost something is mostly reassurance. Tell them what you have written down, and what happens next. Customers who feel heard stop sending a second message.

You will meet customers who lost something precious, such as a wedding ring. For rings, watches and anything with sentimental value, the office wants a phone call, never an email, and the clerk should be told within the hour.

## Weekly sweep

On Friday the clerk reads the log against the shelf. Items with no match after thirty days get a notice in the glass case. Items with no owner after ninety days leave the shelf on the first Monday of the month.

The sweep cannot be done by Quill; it needs hands and shelves. Quill should simply keep the log tidy, so the sweep goes quickly.

## Reports

Each month the office asks how many items were logged and how many returned. The log counts both, and the return rate has stayed near half for two years. Do not quote the rate to customers, since it sounds like a poor chance and the real chance depends on the item.

Phones and keys come back far more often than scarves and gloves.

## Before you close a log line

Check the contact detail twice before you close the line. A wrong digit in a phone number loses the item for good. Do not close a line that has no way to reach the owner.

That is all for lost property.
