# Refund desk hand-over

This skill turns a refund request into a note the desk can act on in one reading. It does not decide refunds and it does not move money.

## When to use it

Use it when the customer says refund, money back, compensation, or "I want my fare back". Do not use it for a plain move to another sailing, which the move tool handles.

If the customer is only asking whether a refund is possible, answer from the policy table below and stop.

## Steps

1. Ask for the six letter booking code.
2. Read the booking and note the sailing date, the fare paid and the number of seats.
3. Ask the reason in one open question.
4. Match the reason to a row of the policy table.
5. Write the hand-over note and send it to the desk queue.
6. Tell the customer the desk answers within two working days.

**Order matters.** Check the booking code against the ledger before you ask about the reason. Customers invent reasons faster when they know no booking is on file.

## Policy table

| Reason | Who decides | What to tell the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Sailing cancelled by the co-op | Desk, automatic | Full refund is the normal outcome |
| Customer ill, doctor's note | Desk lead | Ask them to send the note to the desk address |
| Customer missed the boat | Desk lead | No promise; the desk looks at each case |
| Weather cancel, other boat offered | Desk, automatic | Offer the other boat first |
| Duplicate booking | Desk, automatic | Keep the earlier code, refund the later one |
| Price differs from the card | Desk lead | Collect both prices and the date |

The "Who decides" column is for you, not for the customer. Never read it aloud.

## The hand-over note

Keep the note to six lines. Line one is the code, line two the sailing, line three the amount paid. Line four is the reason in the customer's words, line five the row of the table, and line six what you told them.

Write amounts with the currency sign and two decimals, for example £18.50.

Example note:

> TW4KQP
>
> 16:40 to Skarra, Tuesday
>
> £18.50 for two seats

Notes in the queue are read by tired people at the end of a shift. A note that needs a second question goes to the back of the pile.

## Things that go wrong

- A customer sends a photo of a card → do not open it, ask them to describe the problem in words
- The booking shows two codes for one person → pass both codes to the desk and say which one they quoted
- The fare in the ledger looks lower than the card → write the ledger figure, flag the difference, make no guess
- Customer threatens a complaint → stay level, repeat the two working days, pass the chat to the office
- The ledger tool times out → say so, and ask them to try again in a few minutes

The desk once received forty notes in one morning because a ferry broke down; the queue stayed readable only because every note had the same six lines.

## What the desk does with your note

The desk lead reads the queue at nine and at two. Automatic rows are paid the same day, and lead rows wait for the lead's signature. Refunds go back to the original payment method and nowhere else.

You are not told the outcome. If the customer returns asking, look up the booking and read the desk status line if there is one. If there is none, say that the desk has not answered yet.

## Do not

- Do not offer a voucher in place of a refund
- Do not say a sum is "on its way" before the desk has said so
- Do not argue the policy with the customer
- Never copy a card number into the note, even a partial one

## Before you send

Read the note once more, then send it. A note sent with a typo in the code is returned unread, and the customer waits two more days. The desk will not guess a code from a name.

Rules like these exist because a promised refund that fails costs more goodwill than a refused one.
