# Notes to self

These notes are kept short on purpose. Each one has a date, a fact, and a line on why it matters.

## Customers

**Mrs. Ackerby.** Books the Holm boat every second Thursday for her sister. She is deaf in one ear, so a written reply works better than a phone call. Offer her the aisle seat without being asked.

**The school party.** Twenty-two children and four adults go to Skarra each June. The teacher books them in one block and pays at the window. Never split the block across two sailings; the teacher once lost a child on the quay.

**The tall man from the mainland.** Reports a fault in every booking he makes, and is usually right. Check his fault against the ledger before you dismiss it.

Customers who ring the office twice in a day are not being rude, they are worried. Answer the worry, not the tone.

## Habits that worked

- Repeat the sailing time at the end of every message
- Offer the 08:15 first on Mondays, as it is the only one with room for bicycles
- Name the quay: "Harbour Steps", never "the dock"
- Give the code in groups of three letters, as it is easier to copy
- Wait for the customer's reply before you offer a second option

A reply that offers three options at once usually gets the answer "whichever is cheapest", and that is rarely what they want.

## Things I got wrong

Tuesday: told a customer the Brannock boat was running when the harbour master had paused it for fog. The ledger showed the boat as scheduled, not as running. Check the harbour notice feed as well as the ledger on foggy mornings.

Last month a booking was moved to a sailing already full, because the move tool had accepted the change and showed no warning; the audit line recorded it correctly, but the seat did not exist. The tool's success message only means the ledger was written. Look at the seat count afterwards.

I once quoted a child fare for a sixth-form student. Sixth-formers pay the adult fare from the age of sixteen, whatever school they attend.

## Tools

The move tool has a quirk: it silently drops a seat if the note field contains an ampersand. Keep the note field plain, no ampersands and no brackets.

The sailing list is refreshed at four in the morning, and a sailing added later does not appear until the next day. A customer who asks about a new sailing should be told to ring the office, not told it does not exist.

The waiting list tool never returns more than twenty names; when the list is longer, it still says "20 shown". Do not read that as the total.

## The office

The office opens at seven and closes at half past five. Dilys runs the desk in the morning and Torvald in the afternoon. Both prefer a short note over a long chat transcript.

When Dilys is away the refund desk is slow; mention it to the customer so the wait comes as no surprise.

Torvald dislikes being asked the same thing twice. Read the earlier note in the thread before you pass a new one.

## Seasons

Summer runs from the first of May to the end of September. In July and August the Skarra boat sells out two weeks ahead, and the waiting list fills the day after. Warn customers early; a customer told in June has options, one told in August does not.

Winter timetables drop the water taxi and the evening Holm boat. Say "winter timetable" in the first line when a customer asks about those.

Remember that the harbour is dark by half past four in December.

## Humour

The skippers joke about the weather, and customers join in. Smile at it, and move on to the booking.

> Fog is the harbour's only real timetable.

That line is the old harbour master's, and customers who know him like to hear it.

## Rules I set myself

Look at the seat count before you say a move is done. Say the code aloud in threes, and wait for the customer to confirm. A rushed message is the one that gets a refund request a day later.

Quill does not argue about the weather; the harbour master has the last word on every sailing. Nobody on the quay has ever won that argument.

Never close a chat by saying "see you on the boat", since half our customers are booking for someone else.

Those are all the notes for now.
