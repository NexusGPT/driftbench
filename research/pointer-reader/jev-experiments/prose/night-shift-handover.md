# Night-shift handover

The evening clerk writes this page before leaving, and the morning clerk reads it before the first call. It is short because both of them are tired at the hand-over.

**Why it exists.** The office has no overlap between shifts. Anything not on this page is lost.

## Before you leave

1. Count the till and write the total at the top of the page.
2. List every open refund note and its age in days.
3. Write the state of each boat as the harbour master left it.
4. Lock the blue shelf and the cash drawer.
5. Switch off the quay lights only after the last boat has tied up.

Do the till count with a witness whenever the takings are over a hundred pounds. A second pair of eyes is the cheapest audit there is.

## The board

The whiteboard by the door shows the state of each boat in three colours. Green means running, amber means delayed, and red means cancelled or paused. Wipe it only when the morning clerk has copied it to the page.

| Sign | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Red marker on the Holm boat | Cancelled or paused | Tell every Holm booking |
| Amber on any boat | Delayed | Write the delay on the board |
| Blue ring round a name | Waiting list | Ring that person first |
| No receipt | Takings unclear | Stop and ask the other clerk |
| Card machine offline | Cash only | Put the sign in the window |

## What the morning clerk finds

The office is cold at seven. The heater takes twenty minutes, so put it on first and the kettle second. The cash drawer is locked and the key is on the hook by the board.

Open the log before anything else; a stale log makes everything after it harder. Read the refund notes from oldest to newest. The oldest notes are usually the angriest.

When an entry is unclear, assume the clerk who wrote it knew more than the page shows, and ring them. They would rather be rung than have a customer wait.

## Handing over the phone

The office phone is forwarded to the clerk's mobile after half past five. Move the forward to the next clerk before you leave, since a forward that points to a sleeping person is worse than none.

If a call comes in while you are handing over, take it; the hand-over can wait. Customers on the line are the reason the office exists.

## Emergencies at night

The harbour emergency number rings through to the harbour master's house. It is not for booking questions. Give it only for a person in the water, a fire, or a boat aground, never for a late ferry.

Phone the number yourself if someone on the quay reports an emergency; do not ask the caller to ring. Stay on the line until the harbour master says you can stop.

Case: in February a walker slipped on the quay steps and the clerk on duty rang the number herself while a colleague stayed with the walker; the lifeboat was alongside in six minutes.

## Cash and cards

Takings are counted at close and again at open. A difference of more than two pounds is written on the page and shown to the lead. Small differences are common on Saturdays, and the lead has never found a pattern.

The card machine is a rented device and it connects through the office router. It drops offline whenever the router restarts, which the router does at three in the morning. Check the machine at seven.

Never leave a card receipt face up on the counter overnight.

## Notes for next time

- Print the fare card in March, not April
- Ask the harbour master for the winter pause dates in October
- Replace the blue marker, it is nearly dry
- Check the glass case on Fridays

## Keys

The office has three keys: front door, shelf and drawer. Keep them on the ring by the board, and sign the key book whenever one leaves the building. A key taken home without a signature is a key nobody can find in the morning.

The shelf key opens the blue shelf; do not lend it to a walker who says they only want a look. Hand over the item instead, and keep the key.

The page ends here.
