FRONT MATTER

  2	Cleans exports from banks and till systems before analysis. Use when a file has odd dates, mixed number formats or duplicates.
SECTION (level 1)
  3	# Data cleaning
  PARAGRAPH
    4	Exports from banks and tills are never clean.
    5	Cleaning is half the work and it has to be repeatable.
  SECTION (level 2)
    6	## The order of cleaning
    PARAGRAPH
      7	Work in this order, and do not skip a step:
    LIST
      8	Look at the raw file and count the rows
      9	Fix the encoding and the separators
      10	Parse the dates
      11	Parse the amounts
      12	Remove exact duplicates
      13	Count the rows again and explain the difference
  SECTION (level 2)
    14	## Looking at the file
    PARAGRAPH
      15	Print the first ten rows and the last five before you do anything else.
      16	The last rows often hold totals that must not be treated as data.
    PARAGRAPH
      17	Note the number of rows in the raw file.
      18	You will compare it with the clean file at the end.
  SECTION (level 2)
    19	## Encoding and separators
    PARAGRAPH
      20	Banks in the region export with semicolons and a decimal comma.
      21	Tills export with commas and a decimal point.
      22	Read the file with the separator that matches its source.
    PARAGRAPH
      23	If accented letters look broken, the file is probably in a Western European encoding and not UTF-8.
      24	Reread it with that encoding and do not edit the letters by hand.
  SECTION (level 2)
    25	## Dates
    PARAGRAPH
      26	Parse dates with an explicit format and never with automatic detection.
      27	Automatic detection reads 03/04/2026 as March in some places and April in others.
    PARAGRAPH
      28	If a date does not match the format, keep the row and put the date in a column named date_problem.
      29	Report the number of such rows in your reply.
  SECTION (level 2)
    30	## Amounts
    PARAGRAPH
      31	Remove the currency sign and the thousands separator, then convert the text to a number.
      32	Store the result in cents.
    PARAGRAPH
      33	A minus sign may sit before the number, after it or inside brackets.
      34	Treat all three as negative.
    PARAGRAPH
      35	Never drop a row because its amount is zero.
      36	A zero line may be a cancelled payment that the partners want to see.
  SECTION (level 2)
    37	## Duplicates
    PARAGRAPH
      38	Only exact duplicates are removed: same date, same amount, same description and same reference.
      39	Two payments of the same amount on the same day with different references are two payments.
    PARAGRAPH
      40	If you are unsure whether two rows are duplicates, keep both and say so.
      41	Removing a real payment is worse than keeping a repeated one.
  SECTION (level 2)
    42	## A cleaning log
    PARAGRAPH
      43	Write every change to a log file in the working folder.
      44	The log names the rule, the number of rows touched and one sample row.
    PARAGRAPH
      45	Example log line: parsed dates with the format day-month-year, 412 rows, 3 failed, sample row 88.
  SECTION (level 2)
    46	## Reusing the script
    PARAGRAPH
      47	Save the cleaning script in the working folder with the client code in its name.
      48	The next month starts from the same script.
    PARAGRAPH
      49	Do not change a saved script without noting what changed at the top.
  SECTION (level 2)
    50	## Common traps
    TABLE
      ROW
        51	Trap
        52	What happens
      ROW
        53	A header repeated in the middle of the file
        54	The amount column turns into text
      ROW
        55	A total line at the end
        56	The totals are counted twice
      ROW
        57	Trailing spaces in names
        58	Matching two lists fails silently
    PARAGRAPH
      59	Remember that a file can be correct and still not be the file you were asked for.
    PARAGRAPH
      60	Well done.

=== FILE B-invoice-reconciliation-skill.md ===
FRONT MATTER
  1	invoice-reconciliation
  2	Matches a list of invoices with a list of bank payments and reports what is unpaid or unexplained.
SECTION (level 1)
  3	# Invoice reconciliation
  PARAGRAPH
    4	A reconciliation answers two questions: which invoices have been paid, and which payments have no invoice.
    5	The partners read the answer before they chase a client.
  SECTION (level 2)
    6	## What you need
    PARAGRAPH
      7	You need the invoice list, the bank export and the date to reconcile up to.
      8	The invoice list comes from the accounting package and the bank export from the bank.
    PARAGRAPH
      9	If one of the three is missing, ask for it and do not begin.
      10	A reconciliation on half the data misleads everyone.
  SECTION (level 2)
    11	## Matching rules
    PARAGRAPH
      12	Match in three passes, from the strongest rule to the weakest:
    LIST
      13	Same amount and same invoice number in the description
      14	Same amount and same client name
      15	Same amount within three days of the due date
    PARAGRAPH
      16	A payment can match only one invoice.
      17	Once a payment is matched, take it out of the pool.
    PARAGRAPH
      18	Do not match on the amount alone.
      19	Two invoices of the same amount are common in a small shop.
  SECTION (level 2)
    20	## Partial payments
    PARAGRAPH
      21	A client may pay an invoice in two parts.
      22	If two payments from the same client add up exactly to an invoice, match both to it.
    PARAGRAPH
      23	If the sum is close but not exact, do not match.
      24	List the case under questions for the bookkeeper.
  SECTION (level 2)
    25	## Output sheets
    PARAGRAPH
      26	The workbook has four sheets: matched, unpaid, unexplained and questions.
    TABLE
      ROW
        27	Sheet
        28	Holds
        29	Sorted by
      ROW
        30	matched
        31	Invoices with their payments
        32	Invoice date
      ROW
        33	unpaid
        34	Invoices with no payment
        35	Days overdue, largest first
      ROW
        36	unexplained
        37	Payments with no invoice
        38	Date
      ROW
        39	questions
        40	Cases you could not decide
        41	Amount, largest first
  SECTION (level 2)
    42	## Overdue days
    PARAGRAPH
      43	Count the days overdue from the due date to the reconciliation date.
      44	If the due date is missing, use the invoice date plus thirty days and say so.
    PARAGRAPH
      45	Do not write a chasing message for the client.
      46	The bookkeeper decides how to approach a client.
  SECTION (level 2)
    47	## Checks
    PARAGRAPH
      48	Before you save, check that the total of the matched sheet plus the unpaid sheet equals the total of the invoice list.
      49	Check the same for the payments with the matched and unexplained sheets.
    PARAGRAPH
      50	If a total differs, find the row that is missing or counted twice.
      51	Do not hide the difference in a rounding line.
  SECTION (level 2)
    52	## An example
    PARAGRAPH
      53	Example: invoice 2041 for 480.00 euros was paid in two payments of 240.00 on the 3rd and the 9th.
      54	Both payments went to the matched sheet under invoice 2041.
  SECTION (level 2)
    55	## Notes on clients
    PARAGRAPH
      56	Some clients pay several invoices in one transfer.
      57	The description then lists the invoice numbers.
      58	Split such a payment between the invoices in the order the numbers appear.
    PARAGRAPH
      59	Remember that one client pays on the last day of the month, whatever the due date.
    PARAGRAPH
      60	Good luck.
