# Glossary

New crew members hear these words on their first morning.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pinnock | A small round dough ball that is the start of a loaf |
| Sconce room | The small cupboard behind the till that holds the keys |
| Plinth | The steel bench under the east window where racks cool |
| Wobbin | The name of the sourdough starter |
| Tarn flour | The coarse flour from the hill mill |

## Usage

Use the word pinnock for the dough ball and never for a finished loaf. Do not call the sconce room the safe, because it holds keys and not money. For example, write that the opening crew carried six pinnocks down, not six loaves. The word plinth comes from the old stone bench that once stood there. Ask if you hear a word that is not here.

## Short forms

- OB means the order book
- CB means the closing book
- SB means the shift book
- Spell out a short form the first time you use it in a note
- Do not use a short form on a customer label

## Naming loaves

Give a new loaf a name of one or two words, and tell the head baker before you use it. Check the name against the allergen folder before it goes on a tag. The best known loaf is the Tarn Rye, which sells out by ten. Case: a loaf called Nut Free Rye was named by a new crew member and had to be renamed the same day.
