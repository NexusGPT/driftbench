# Opening the shop

The opening crew arrives at half past five and works until the doors open at seven. Every morning follows the same order, and the order matters more than the speed.

## Lamps and doors

Switch on the front lamps before you unlock the street door. Unlock the street door with the brass key that hangs in the sconce room. Never prop the back door open with a crate while the ovens are warm. The sconce room is the small cupboard behind the till, and it holds every key. Check that the alarm panel shows a green light, then write the time in the opening book. For example, if the panel shows an amber light, the night crew left a window open.

## Ovens

Light the big oven first and the small oven ten minutes later. Wait until the big oven reads two hundred and twenty degrees before the first tray goes in. The big oven holds twelve trays and the small oven holds four. Do not load a cold tray into a hot oven, because the glaze will crack. Case: a tray of pinnocks went in cold on a Monday and came out with split tops.

## The dough loft

- Carry the proved dough down from the dough loft in the wheeled bin
- Weigh each pinnock on the brass scale before it is shaped
- Do not stack more than three bins on the lift
- The loft is warmest near the east window

## Final checks

Count the float in the till before you open the street door. Fill the front shelves from left to right, oldest loaves first. Customers usually form a line by ten past seven. Thank you for opening well. Good luck today.
