# The dough loft

A pinnock is the small round dough ball that every loaf at Crumbwick starts from. The loft has a proving drawer, a mixing trough and a brass scale.

## Mixing

Mix the tarn flour with cold water and let it rest for twenty minutes. Add the salt only after the flour has rested. Never use hot water in the trough, because it kills the starter. For example, a trough mixed with hot water on a summer day produced flat loaves for a whole week. The starter is called Wobbin and it lives in the green crock on the top shelf. Feed Wobbin with a spoon of flour and a spoon of water every evening.

## Proving

- Place the shaped pinnocks on the linen in the proving drawer
- Close the proving drawer before you start the heater
- Do not open the proving drawer more than once an hour
- The proving drawer holds a steady warmth of twenty six degrees
- A pinnock that has doubled in size after ninety minutes is ready for the oven

## Shaping

Shape each pinnock with two hands and a light dusting of flour. Do not press the dough flat, because the crumb will turn dense. Weigh each pinnock again after shaping and put any that are light back on the scale. A standard pinnock weighs four hundred and fifty grams. Case: a light pinnock of four hundred grams baked into a loaf that the shop could not sell as standard.

## Cleaning the loft

Scrape the trough with the plastic scraper and rinse it with cold water. Do not wash the linen with soap, because the smell passes into the crumb. Ask Marek if the starter smells odd.
