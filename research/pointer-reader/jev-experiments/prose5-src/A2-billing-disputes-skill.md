# Billing disputes{{n}}

This skill covers questions about the monthly bill.{{d}} It does not decide refunds and it does not move money.{{d}}

## When to use it{{n}}

Use it when the customer says bill, charge, refund, overcharged or "I was billed twice".{{d}} Do not use it for a price quote on a new plan; that goes to sales.{{p}}

## What a bill contains{{n}}

Every bill has four parts: the plan fee, the equipment rental, any one-off charges and the credits.{{d}} The plan fee is charged in advance and the other lines in arrears.{{d}} A credit is shown as a negative number.{{d}}

Read the bill line by line before you answer.{{g}} Do not guess which line the customer means.{{p}}

## First reply to a dispute{{n}}

Thank the customer for the details and say you are looking at the bill.{{r#a}} Name the line you are checking, so they know you read it.{{r#a}}

If the line is a charge the customer agreed to, explain it in one or two sentences.{{r#b}} Say when they agreed to it and where it is written down.{{r#b}}

If the line looks wrong, do not argue and do not promise a refund.{{p}} Open a billing ticket and say that the billing team decides.{{r}}

## What to tell the customer{{n}}

| Situation{{n}} | What to tell the customer{{n}} |
|---|---|
| The charge matches the plan{{r}} | Explain the line and offer to send a copy of the plan terms{{r}} |
| A charge appears twice{{r}} | Say it looks like a duplicate and that billing will check it within five working days{{r}} |
| A credit is missing{{r}} | Say you have asked billing to look for it and give the ticket number{{r}} |
| The customer disputes the plan fee{{p}} | Do not discuss the price; say the billing team will contact them{{p}} |

## Tone in a dispute{{n}}

Customers who dispute a bill are often upset about the money, not about you.{{d}} Answer the worry, not the tone.{{r}}

Never say the customer is wrong.{{p}} Say what the bill shows and what you will do.{{r}}

If the customer swears, stay polite and carry on.{{r}} A customer who is abusive three times in a row gets one warning and then a handover to the duty manager.{{r#c}} Tell them you are passing the conversation on.{{r#c}}

## Writing the ticket{{n}}

A good billing ticket has five fields:{{r#d}}

- The invoice number{{r#d}}
- The line in dispute{{r#d}}
- What the customer expects{{r#d}}
- What you already told them{{r#d}}
- The date of the first complaint{{r#d}}

Copy the invoice number exactly as printed.{{r}} A wrong number costs the billing team an hour.{{d}}

Do not paste the whole conversation into the ticket.{{p}} Write a summary of three lines at most.{{p}}

## Refund wording{{n}}

You never say a refund is approved.{{p}} Billing approves refunds and sends a confirmation by email.{{d}}

Example: "I have asked the billing team to look at the second charge.{{e}} They will reply by email within five working days, and your ticket number is QB-4471."{{e}}

## Special cases{{n}}

Customers on a social tariff are charged half the plan fee.{{d}} Explain this if they ask why their bill is lower than a neighbour's.{{r}}

A customer who has just moved house may see two plan fees on one bill.{{d}} In that case check the move date before you open a ticket.{{g#e}} If the move date falls inside the billing month, the double fee is correct for that month.{{g#e}}

Remember that the billing team works Monday to Friday.{{d}} A dispute opened on Saturday is read on Monday.{{d}}

## Checks before you send{{n}}

Before you send a billing reply, check that:{{g#f}}

- You named the line in dispute{{g#f}}
- You gave a ticket number{{g#f}}
- You promised nothing about the result{{g#f}}

Close with the next step and who owns it.{{r}}

Thank you.{{n}}
