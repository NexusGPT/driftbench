# Outage handling{{n}}

An outage is a fault the customer cannot fix by themselves.{{d}} Most reports turn out to be a router that needs a restart.{{d}}

## Step 1: check the line{{n}}

Open the line monitor for the customer's address before you ask them to do anything.{{g}} The monitor shows one of three states: up, degraded or down.{{d}}

If the monitor shows down and the network team has a known outage for the street, tell the customer at once.{{r#a}} Give the outage reference and the estimated fix time from the network team's note.{{r#a}}

If the monitor shows up, the fault is probably inside the house.{{d}} Go on to step 2.{{r}}

## Step 2: the usual causes{{n}}

Ask the customer these questions, one at a time:{{r#b}}

- Are the lights on the router lit{{r#b}}
- Is the network cable firmly in the white socket{{r#b}}
- Does the problem affect every device or only one{{r#b}}

Ask one question per message.{{p}} Two questions in one message get half an answer.{{d}}

Wait for the customer's reply before you ask the next question.{{g}}

## Step 3: restart{{n}}

Ask the customer to switch the router off at the wall, wait thirty seconds and switch it on again.{{g}} It takes about three minutes to come back.{{d}}

Do not ask the customer to reset the router to factory settings.{{p}} A factory reset wipes the Wi-Fi name and the password, and most customers cannot set them again.{{d}}

## What the lights mean{{n}}

| Light{{n}} | Meaning{{n}} |
|---|---|
| Steady green{{d}} | The router is online{{d}} |
| Flashing green{{d}} | The router is starting{{d}} |
| Steady red{{d}} | No signal from the street{{d}} |
| Off{{d}} | No power{{d}} |

## When the restart does not help{{n}}

If the lights show steady red, open a network ticket.{{r#c}} Add the address, the time the fault began and the answers you already have.{{r#c}}

If the lights are off, ask the customer to try another wall socket.{{r}} A dead socket is the cause more often than a dead router.{{d}}

If every light is normal and the internet is still slow, run the speed check in the line monitor.{{r}} Compare the result with the plan speed on the account.{{r}}

## Booking a visit{{n}}

Book a technician only after the restart and the socket test have failed.{{g}} A visit that finds nothing is billed to the customer after the second time.{{d}}

Offer the two next free morning slots and the two next free afternoon slots.{{r}} Do not offer evening slots; technicians do not work in the evening.{{p}}

Confirm the slot by repeating the day, the time window and the address.{{r}} Say that the technician will ring thirty minutes before arriving.{{r}}

## Vulnerable customers{{n}}

A customer is vulnerable if the account carries the care flag.{{d}} Vulnerable customers get the first free slot, whatever the day.{{r}} Mark their ticket urgent without waiting to be asked.{{r}}

## Wording{{n}}

Say "the line is down in your street" when the monitor shows a street outage.{{r}} Say "the line looks fine from here" when it does not.{{r}}

Avoid saying "it should be fixed soon".{{p}} Give the estimate or say there is none.{{r}}

Example: "I can see a fault on your street.{{e}} Our network team expects to fix it by six this evening, and I will update your ticket if that changes."{{e}}

Last of all, remember that the customer cannot reach you if the connection is down.{{d}} If they write by phone message, answer there.{{r}}

Good luck.{{n}}
