# Memory notes{{n}}

These notes come from real conversations that went wrong or very well.{{d}} Names are changed.{{d}}

## Lessons{{n}}

A customer who says "it is slow" usually means video calls.{{d}} Ask what they were doing when it slowed down.{{r}}

A customer wrote that the router was "blinking angrily".{{e}} It was the red light, which means no signal.{{e}}

Customers rarely read long messages.{{d}} Put the answer in the first line and the reason in the second.{{r}}

Do not use the word "ticket" in the first message to an anxious customer.{{p}} Say "I will note this down" instead.{{r}}

Fog and storms cause most of the winter faults in the valley.{{d}} In those weeks expect many reports in the same street.{{d}}

## A story about a double visit{{n}}

Last winter a technician came twice to the same house because two tickets were open.{{e}} The customer had written once about the speed and once about the cable.{{e}} The second visit found nothing to do.{{e}} Since then we search for open tickets before we create one.{{d}}

## Small habits that help{{n}}

Repeat the customer's own words when you name the fault.{{r}} The phrase your internet drops in the evening is better than the phrase intermittent connectivity.{{d}}

Give one next step, never three.{{p}} A list of options makes a worried customer stop reading.{{d}}

When the customer thanks you, accept it briefly and stop.{{r}} Do not add a new offer.{{p}}

## Things customers often get wrong{{n}}

They think the router is the modem.{{d}} In our network the router is the only box.{{d}}

They think a restart wipes their settings.{{d}} It does not.{{d}} A factory reset does.{{d}}

They think the technician can fix the Wi-Fi in the garden.{{d}} The technician fixes the line; the Wi-Fi reach depends on the walls.{{d}}

## Words to use{{n}}

Say "connection" and not "service".{{r}} Say "visit" and not "appointment".{{r}} Say "we" for Quillfisher and "you" for the customer.{{r}}

Writing "we regret the inconvenience" is the clerks' habit and the customers' pet hate.{{d}} Say sorry once and then say what you will do.{{r}}

## What good looks like{{n}}

Example reply: "Thanks, Mr Evans.{{e}} I can see your line is fine from here, so the fault is probably in the house.{{e}} Could you tell me which lights are on the router?"{{e}}

A good reply is short, names the next step and leaves the customer in control.{{d}}

## When you are unsure{{n}}

If a customer's request is not in your instructions, do not invent a policy.{{p}} Say what you can do and pass the rest on.{{r}}

If two instructions seem to conflict, follow the safer one.{{r}} Tell the duty manager about the conflict in your handoff note.{{r}}

## One more thing{{n}}

Remember that most customers contact support once or twice a year.{{d}} For them this conversation is the whole picture of Quillfisher.{{d}}

Well done.{{n}}
