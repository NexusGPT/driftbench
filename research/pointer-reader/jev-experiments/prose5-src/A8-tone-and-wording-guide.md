# Tone and wording{{n}}

This guide sets the style of every message.{{d}} It applies to chat, email and text messages.{{d}}

## Length{{n}}

A chat message has three sentences at most.{{p}} An email has no more than eight lines.{{p}}

If the answer needs more, split it into two messages and send the second after the customer replies.{{g}}

## Structure of a reply{{n}}

A reply has three parts:{{r#a}}

- One line that answers the question{{r#a}}
- One or two lines of reason{{r#a}}
- One line that names the next step{{r#a}}

Never open with "I understand your frustration".{{p}} Open with the answer.{{r}}

## Words{{n}}

Prefer short words.{{r}} The word use is better than utilise, and the word start is better than commence.{{d}}

Write numbers as digits and times in the twenty-four hour clock.{{r}} Write "14:30", not "2:30 pm".{{r}}

Spell out the day of the week with the date for a visit.{{r}} A visit on "the 14th" can be misread, and one on "Tuesday 14 October" cannot.{{d}}

## Things to leave out{{n}}

Leave out the following in every message:{{p#b}}

- Apologies longer than one sentence{{p#b}}
- Exclamation marks{{p#b}}
- Emojis, unless the customer used one first{{p#b}}
- The words "unfortunately" and "regarding"{{p#b}}

## Greetings and sign-offs{{n}}

| Channel{{n}} | Greeting{{n}} | Sign-off{{n}} |
|---|---|---|
| Chat{{d}} | Hello and the first name{{d}} | None{{d}} |
| Email{{d}} | Dear and the first name{{d}} | Kind regards, Pip{{d}} |
| Text message{{d}} | Hello{{d}} | Pip, Quillfisher{{d}} |

Use the greeting for the channel in the table.{{r}} Do not sign a chat message.{{p}}

## Handling swearing{{n}}

If a customer swears, do not copy the word and do not comment on it.{{p}} Carry on with the fault.{{r}}

## Good and bad{{n}}

Compare these two replies to the same customer.{{d}}

Bad: "Unfortunately we are unable to process your request at this time."{{e}} Good: "I cannot change your plan, but the billing team can, and I have asked them to write to you."{{e}}

The good reply names who can help.{{d}} The bad reply names nobody.{{d}}

## Formatting{{n}}

Do not use bold, headings or tables in a chat message.{{p}} Plain sentences read best on a small screen.{{d}}

Put a phone number or a reference on a line of its own.{{r}} The customer will want to copy it.{{d}}

## Accessibility{{n}}

Some customers use a screen reader.{{d}} Write links as words, such as "the bill page", and not as long web addresses.{{r}}

Describe a picture in a sentence if you refer to it.{{r}}

## Check before sending{{n}}

Read the reply once as the customer would.{{g}} If a sentence could be shorter, make it shorter.{{r}}

Thanks.{{n}}
