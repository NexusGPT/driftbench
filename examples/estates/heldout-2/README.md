# heldout-2

A second held-out estate for the numeric-limit producer: a fictional bicycle repair shop support desk (Spoke, Tandem Lane Cycle Works) with eight prose files in the layout of `heldout-1` (one standing file, four skills, three references). It was written from invention, with `heldout-1` and the truth schema of the gold estates as the only format models, and without reading the producer, its prompts, its tests or its recordings. No model was called.

`truth.yaml` holds, in this order:
- `units`: every unit of the prose with its id (the first ten hex digits of `sha1(file + "\n" + text)`), its file and its text. A unit is a sentence, a list item or one table row; headings and the front matter are not units, and neither is the separator row of a table. The header row of a table is a unit and states no number. A bold lead and the sentence after it stay one unit, as in `heldout-1`.
- `limits`: 64 entries on 57 units (a range is two entries on one unit). Each has the comparison (`lt`, `le`, `gt`, `ge`), the role (`constraint` or `trigger`), what the number counts, and the unit it governs. The comparison follows the sentence, in the normal form of the gold estates: a prohibition may be written as a trigger or as the complementary constraint.
- `non_limits`: 32 numbers that are not limits (counts of listed items, dates, versions, codes, ports, examples, prices quoted as facts, a display size, statistics).
- `refers_to`: the units that point at another unit for their number, 6 entries, each with the unit it points at. The pointers are worded in six ways: a sentence two ahead, a row of a table in the same file, a row of a table in another file, the sentence before, an item of the list below and a note at the foot of the file. The limit sits on the unit that holds the number, and `governs` names the pointing unit. A pointer states no limit of its own. A table that a pointer names is not in `limits_table`; its rows that no pointer names keep their own limit and govern themselves.
- `limits_table`: the 3 standalone tables whose rows state limits (no rule refers to them), each with its header unit and its data rows by unit id (10 rows in all, all with a limit).
- `hard_cases`: the unit ids of the limits that are hard by kind: `table` (a number in a table row, the header says what it limits), `pointer` (the number sits in a unit that another unit points at), `spelled` (a count in words: "one", "twice", "four"), `negative` (stated by "never", "do not", "no more than", "may not"), `range`, `ordinal` ("a second move", "a sixth conversation") and `about-numbers` (a rule about how numbers are written).

Fictional data only.
