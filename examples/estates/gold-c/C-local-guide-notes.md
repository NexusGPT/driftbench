# Local guide notes

These notes are facts, not instructions. Share them when a guest asks, and only then.

## Getting here

The nearest station is Saltby, eleven minutes from the Sea Room by taxi. There are four trains a day and the last one leaves at nine in the evening.

The village bus runs on weekdays only. A guest who arrives on a Sunday needs a taxi.

If a guest asks for a taxi number, give the one on the staff sheet. Do not recommend a different firm.

## Eating

The Anchor serves food until eight and is closed on Mondays. The harbour kiosk sells fish and chips from noon until the light goes. The farm shop sells picnic boxes if they are ordered the evening before.

Breakfast is served between half past seven and half past nine. A guest who wants it earlier can ask the front desk.

## Walking

The coast path runs past all three guesthouses. The stretch to the lighthouse takes about two hours and has no shelter.

Remind walkers to check the tide table before they set out along the sands. The tide comes in fast near the point.

Do not suggest a route to a guest who says they are not used to walking. Offer the shorter loop through the village instead.

## Weather

Winds are strong in autumn and spring. A guest who asks about the weather should be told to check the forecast on the day. Never promise good weather.

## Beaches and dogs

Dogs are allowed on the east beach all year. On the west beach they are banned from May to September.

Tell a guest with a dog about the west beach rule. Many do not know it.

## Events

The harbour has a small market on the first Saturday of each month. The lifeboat open day is in August. Dates change, so say "usually" and send the guest to the village page for the exact day.

Example: "The market is usually on the first Saturday of the month. The village page has the date for your stay."

## Emergencies in the area

The coastguard number is on the staff sheet. If a walker is in trouble on the cliffs, tell the guest to call 999 and ask for the coastguard.

Do not give directions to the rescue point on the cliffs. The emergency services use their own.

Enjoy the coast.
