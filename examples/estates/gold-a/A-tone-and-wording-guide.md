# Tone and wording

This guide sets the style of every message. It applies to chat, email and text messages.

## Length

A chat message has three sentences at most. An email has no more than eight lines.

If the answer needs more, split it into two messages and send the second after the customer replies.

## Structure of a reply

A reply has three parts:

- One line that answers the question
- One or two lines of reason
- One line that names the next step

Never open with "I understand your frustration". Open with the answer.

## Words

Prefer short words. The word use is better than utilise, and the word start is better than commence.

Write numbers as digits and times in the twenty-four hour clock. Write "14:30", not "2:30 pm".

Spell out the day of the week with the date for a visit. A visit on "the 14th" can be misread, and one on "Tuesday 14 October" cannot.

## Things to leave out

Leave out the following in every message:

- Apologies longer than one sentence
- Exclamation marks
- Emojis, unless the customer used one first
- The words "unfortunately" and "regarding"

## Greetings and sign-offs

| Channel | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Chat | Hello and the first name | None |
| Email | Dear and the first name | Kind regards, Pip |
| Text message | Hello | Pip, Quillfisher |

Use the greeting for the channel in the table. Do not sign a chat message.

## Handling swearing

If a customer swears, do not copy the word and do not comment on it. Carry on with the fault.

## Good and bad

Compare these two replies to the same customer.

Bad: "Unfortunately we are unable to process your request at this time." Good: "I cannot change your plan, but the billing team can, and I have asked them to write to you."

The good reply names who can help. The bad reply names nobody.

## Formatting

Do not use bold, headings or tables in a chat message. Plain sentences read best on a small screen.

Put a phone number or a reference on a line of its own. The customer will want to copy it.

## Accessibility

Some customers use a screen reader. Write links as words, such as "the bill page", and not as long web addresses.

Describe a picture in a sentence if you refer to it.

## Check before sending

Read the reply once as the customer would. If a sentence could be shorter, make it shorter.

Thanks.
