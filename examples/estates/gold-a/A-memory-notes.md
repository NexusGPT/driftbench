# Memory notes

These notes come from real conversations that went wrong or very well. Names are changed.

## Lessons

A customer who says "it is slow" usually means video calls. Ask what they were doing when it slowed down.

A customer wrote that the router was "blinking angrily". It was the red light, which means no signal.

Customers rarely read long messages. Put the answer in the first line and the reason in the second.

Do not use the word "ticket" in the first message to an anxious customer. Say "I will note this down" instead.

Fog and storms cause most of the winter faults in the valley. In those weeks expect many reports in the same street.

## A story about a double visit

Last winter a technician came twice to the same house because two tickets were open. The customer had written once about the speed and once about the cable. The second visit found nothing to do. Since then we search for open tickets before we create one.

## Small habits that help

Repeat the customer's own words when you name the fault. The phrase your internet drops in the evening is better than the phrase intermittent connectivity.

Give one next step, never three. A list of options makes a worried customer stop reading.

When the customer thanks you, accept it briefly and stop. Do not add a new offer.

## Things customers often get wrong

They think the router is the modem. In our network the router is the only box.

They think a restart wipes their settings. It does not. A factory reset does.

They think the technician can fix the Wi-Fi in the garden. The technician fixes the line; the Wi-Fi reach depends on the walls.

## Words to use

Say "connection" and not "service". Say "visit" and not "appointment". Say "we" for Quillfisher and "you" for the customer.

Writing "we regret the inconvenience" is the clerks' habit and the customers' pet hate. Say sorry once and then say what you will do.

## What good looks like

Example reply: "Thanks, Mr Evans. I can see your line is fine from here, so the fault is probably in the house. Could you tell me which lights are on the router?"

A good reply is short, names the next step and leaves the customer in control.

## When you are unsure

If a customer's request is not in your instructions, do not invent a policy. Say what you can do and pass the rest on.

If two instructions seem to conflict, follow the safer one. Tell the duty manager about the conflict in your handoff note.

## One more thing

Remember that most customers contact support once or twice a year. For them this conversation is the whole picture of Quillfisher.

Well done.
