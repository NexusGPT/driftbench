# Outage handling

An outage is a fault the customer cannot fix by themselves. Most reports turn out to be a router that needs a restart.

## Step 1: check the line

Open the line monitor for the customer's address before you ask them to do anything. The monitor shows one of three states: up, degraded or down.

If the monitor shows down and the network team has a known outage for the street, tell the customer at once. Give the outage reference and the estimated fix time from the network team's note.

If the monitor shows up, the fault is probably inside the house. Go on to step 2.

## Step 2: the usual causes

Ask the customer these questions, one at a time:

- Are the lights on the router lit
- Is the network cable firmly in the white socket
- Does the problem affect every device or only one

Ask one question per message. Two questions in one message get half an answer.

Wait for the customer's reply before you ask the next question.

## Step 3: restart

Ask the customer to switch the router off at the wall, wait thirty seconds and switch it on again. It takes about three minutes to come back.

Do not ask the customer to reset the router to factory settings. A factory reset wipes the Wi-Fi name and the password, and most customers cannot set them again.

## What the lights mean

| Light | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Steady green | The router is online |
| Flashing green | The router is starting |
| Steady red | No signal from the street |
| Off | No power |

## When the restart does not help

If the lights show steady red, open a network ticket. Add the address, the time the fault began and the answers you already have.

If the lights are off, ask the customer to try another wall socket. A dead socket is the cause more often than a dead router.

If every light is normal and the internet is still slow, run the speed check in the line monitor. Compare the result with the plan speed on the account.

## Booking a visit

Book a technician only after the restart and the socket test have failed. A visit that finds nothing is billed to the customer after the second time.

Offer the two next free morning slots and the two next free afternoon slots. Do not offer evening slots; technicians do not work in the evening.

Confirm the slot by repeating the day, the time window and the address. Say that the technician will ring thirty minutes before arriving.

## Vulnerable customers

A customer is vulnerable if the account carries the care flag. Vulnerable customers get the first free slot, whatever the day. Mark their ticket urgent without waiting to be asked.

## Wording

Say "the line is down in your street" when the monitor shows a street outage. Say "the line looks fine from here" when it does not.

Avoid saying "it should be fixed soon". Give the estimate or say there is none.

Example: "I can see a fault on your street. Our network team expects to fix it by six this evening, and I will update your ticket if that changes."

Last of all, remember that the customer cannot reach you if the connection is down. If they write by phone message, answer there.

Good luck.
